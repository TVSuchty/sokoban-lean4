## 1. Why Are Sokoban Solvers All About Deadlocks?

In Sokoban, you push boxes (`$`) onto goals (`.`). There is one crucial rule that makes Sokoban brutally different from sliding-tile puzzles:

**You can push boxes, but you can NEVER pull them.**

That means a single careless push can permanently ruin a puzzle even though 99% of the board still looks fine. An automated solver (like Breadth-First Search or A*) explores huge state trees—and if it doesn't recognize dead-end states early, it wastes 99.9% of its time pushing boxes around in hopeless states.

So solver authors invent **deadlock detectors**: fast checks that look at a board state and say *"Deadlock! Prune this branch!"*

### The Catch: A Single False Positive Destroys Everything
A deadlock detector has one non-negotiable job: **Soundness**.
* If it says **"Deadlock"** on a state whose remaining winning paths all pass through it (or on the only winning path), the solver throws away every winning continuation and falsely claims the puzzle is impossible!
* On the other hand, saying **"Not sure"** (`false`) on a hopeless state is safe (it just means the solver keeps searching a bit longer).

This is why informal "obvious" heuristics are dangerous in Sokoban—and why formalizing them in Lean 4 is so rewarding!

## 2. The Ladder of Verified Deadlock Detectors

Inside this repo, we build and **mathematically prove** a hierarchy of stronger and stronger deadlock checks:

### Level 1: Corner Deadlock (`cornerDeadlock_sound`)
```text
#####
#$. #   <- Box $ at top-left has a wall UP and a wall LEFT,
#@  #      and it is NOT on a goal. It can NEVER move again!
#####
```
Even ignoring all other boxes, no box can ever be pushed into a goal if it is pinned on one horizontal side and one vertical side.

### Level 2: Simple Dead Squares (`deadSquareDeadlock_sound`)
```text
######
#@ $ #  <- Box $ is NOT in a corner (left and right are empty!).
# ## #     Yet the continuous wall ABOVE prevents pushing any box down
#  . #     off the top row, and the corners at both ends are dead.
######
```
Using backward Breadth-First Search (`computeLiveSquares`) from all goals, we compute every square from which a single box *could* reach a goal even if all other boxes vanished. Any square outside `live` is a **Simple Dead Square**. If any box sits on one, the state is provably unsolvable.

### Level 3: Classic 2×2 Block & Freeze Deadlocks (`block2x2Deadlock_sound`, `classicFreezeDeadlock_sound`)
```text
######         ######
# $$ #         #.  .#
# $$ #         ##$  #   <- Zig-zag freeze! Neither box is in a
# @..#         # $###      corner or on a dead square, yet each
#  ..#         # @  #      blocks the other permanently!
######         ######
```
Even if every box sits on a live square, boxes can **freeze each other**! A set `F` of boxes is frozen if every box in `F` is blocked on at least one side of each axis by walls or other boxes in `F`. If any box in `F` is not on a goal, it can never reach a goal.

### Level 4: `SuperFreeze` — Combining Dead Squares & Player Reachability (`superFreezeDeadlock_sound`)
Look at this 5×6 puzzle (`exPlayerTrapBoard`):
```text
#####
#   #   <- Top corridor has NO goals -> every cell here is a Simple Dead Square!
##$##   <- Box $ sits right above the goal . !
# . #
# @ #   <- Player @ is trapped below the box.
#####
```
Wait:
* Is `$` in a corner? **No.**
* Is `$` on a Simple Dead Square? **No**—pushing it down reaches `.`!
* Is `$` locally frozen by walls/boxes? **No**—both above and below `$` are empty floor!

Yet the puzzle is **completely unsolvable**! Why?
* Pushing `$` **up** puts it onto a Simple Dead Square.
* Pushing `$` **down** onto `.` requires the player `@` to walk to the square *above* `$`—which is impossible without pushing `$` first!

Our unified theorem **`superFreeze_sound`** handles all four blocking reasons simultaneously (walls/frozen boxes, Simple Dead Squares, blocked pushing sides, and topological player cutoffs via `CanWalkAvoiding`) inside one greatest-fixed-point iteration (`computeSuperFrozen`).

## 3. Three "Obvious" Heuristics—and Lean's Counterexamples!

In `Sokoban/Counterexamples.lean`, we test three tempting heuristics (plausible solver-pruning mistakes) and let Lean **prove all three are wrong**:

### Counterexample 1: "Treat Simple Dead Squares Like Walls on Both Sides" (`naiveDeadSquareFreeze_unsound`)
```text
#####
#@$.#
#####
```
* `(1,1)` (where `@` stands!) is a Simple Dead Square (it's a corner).
* A naive heuristic checks if `$` is blocked on left/right by `wall ∨ deadSquare` and cries **DEADLOCK!**
* **Why it's wrong:** The *destination* of a push must be live, NOT the square where the player stands to push! Simply pushing `.right` solves the board in 1 move!

### Counterexample 2: "Check If the Player Can Reach the Pushing Side Avoiding All Current Boxes" (`naiveCurrentReachFreeze_unsound`)
```text
######
###@##
#.$$##
### ##
###.##
######
```
* Look at the left box `$` at `(2,2)`. Right now, player `@` at `(3,1)` is sealed in by the right box `$` at `(3,2)` and walls—so `@` **cannot** reach either pushing side of the left box!
* A static reachability check marks the left box frozen and cries **DEADLOCK!**
* **Why it's wrong:** The right box isn't frozen! Pushing it `.down, .down` onto `.` clears the path, then `.up, .left` solves the entire puzzle in 4 moves. `computeSuperFrozen` iteratively *unfreezes* the right box first, then sees `@` can walk through `(3,2)` to push the left box!

### Counterexample 3: "Never Push a Box Off a Goal" (`naiveGoalLock_unsound`)
```text
########
#   ####
#@$ * .#
########
```
* Box `*` is already sitting on goal `(4,2)`! Treating `*` as a permanent wall—or even just forbidding pushes from a goal onto *non-goal floor* (`StepNoOffGoalPush`)—makes the puzzle mathematically unsolvable (both proved in Lean!).
* **Why it's wrong:** In real Sokoban, you walk around the top bypass, push `*` off `(4,2)` onto the non-goal floor `(5,2)` and then onto `.` at `(6,2)`, and finally push `$` into the vacated goal `(4,2)` (14 moves total).
